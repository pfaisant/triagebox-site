How Gmail → Calendar actually runs

  1. Install and connect Gmail. Connect Calendar when you want creates. No TriageBox account.
  2. .ics path. If the message carries a calendar invite, use Add to calendar on the card. Times come from the file, not the model.
  3. Prose path. Summarize with your Gemini, xAI, or OpenRouter key. When the summary looks like a dated event and there is no .ics, Add to calendar extracts one event and creates it — no confirm step in between.
  4. Amend or Remove. The card keeps the event you just added. Clearing a wrong AI field erases it. Remove deletes it on Google Calendar and offers Add again.

Limits, stated

What this page does not claim

  • It does not invent times. An .ics is exact. A prose extraction can be wrong — check Amend.
  • It does not add every date in the email. One event from the summary.
  • It does not run the AI path without your key, or without Calendar connected.
  • It does not offer AI Add to calendar beside a real .ics — the local chip is enough.
  • It does not create on a secondary calendar. Creates go to primary.
  • It does not replace the Calendar tab agenda (Today / Upcoming for the next 30 days).
  • It does not snooze mail or list unanswered sent threads. Those are snooze until reply and waiting for reply.
  • It does not summarize PDFs. That is Gmail PDF summary.
  • It does not run on your phone.
  • It does not send mail through a TriageBox server.
  • It is not for anyone else’s mailbox.

Quick answers

Gmail to Google Calendar

How do I create a Google Calendar event from a Gmail email?

Gmail can open an .ics invite or you can copy times into Calendar by hand. TriageBox 1.57 is a Chrome extension with two paths on the card: an .ics attachment gets a local Add to calendar chip (exact times, no AI). If the thread summary looks like a dated event and there is no .ics, Add to calendar extracts one event with your AI key and creates it on your primary calendar in one click. Amend and Remove stay on the card. The model can be wrong.

Do I need an AI key?

Not for a real .ics invite — that parses in your browser. For a plain email that only describes a meeting, yes: summarize first with your Gemini, xAI, or OpenRouter key, then use Add to calendar. Calendar OAuth is also required for create, amend, and remove.

Is this the same as snooze or waiting for reply?

No. Snooze hides a thread until a time or a reply. Waiting for reply lists sent mail nobody answered. This page only puts an event on Google Calendar. See snooze until reply and waiting for reply for those jobs.

Does my mail go through a TriageBox server?

No. There is no developer-operated backend. .ics parsing stays in your browser. Optional AI goes from your browser to the provider whose key you added. Calendar writes go to Google Calendar under your OAuth.
